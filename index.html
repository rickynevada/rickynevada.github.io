Welcome to my portfolio website!

I've been using computers since my Dad brought home an Epson Equity when I was a kid, and I have been hooked on electronics ever since. As a child and early teenager I would tinker around in DOS, Windows 3.1, Windows 95, and finally XP installing software, configuring operating systems, and upgrading hardware like RAM, graphics cards, and modems. In high school, when Windows XP was in its prime, I was creating websites, designing video game levels for Duke Nukem 3D, computer drafting, and connecting friends to networks at LAN parties.

Through high school I worked as a licensed title clerk for a license agency in Washington State, where I transferred titles and renewed registrations. Sweet gig for a 16 year old. During my last year of high school I worked at a local machine shop where I learned to use a manual mill and lathe, CNC mill, learned all about tooling, and did some CNC programming in BobCAD.

After high school I enlisted in the US Navy as an electronics technician, where I spent six years learning to fix, repair, and troubleshoot countless different types of communication and RADAR systems -- among other activities like damage control, seamanship, armed security, and the custodial arts. I was formally trained on the WSC-6(v)9 Super High Frequency (SHF) satellite communication system that was responsible for delivering secured and non-secure internet to my ship's crew aboard the USS Ramage DDG-61, on which I spent 4 years living on board. I also worked on some other radios, HF, VHF, and UHF, and their associated antenna systems in addition to interior communication systems. Finally, I went to a month long soldering and PCB repair school.

At some point during my service I became interested in learning how many of the systems I worked on were designed. Therefore, when my enlistment was up, I separated and came back home. With the financial support of the post 9/11 GI Bill, I received my bachelor's degree in electrical engineering with a minor in computer & software systems (computer science). For my last two years of undergrad, I picked up a job at a local application specific integrated circuit (ASIC) company working 30+ hours a week. There I worked with ASIC designers and engineers to validate ASICs. Between the Arduino I picked up at RadioShack around this time, and the ASIC job, this is when I began getting interested in microcontrollers and embedded systems.

Through my senior year in undergrad I focused more on embedded software, selecting projects and classes that fueled my interest. By the time I finished, I'd worked with several different microcontroller tool chains (TI, Microchip, Atmel, and Cypress PSoC), developed single-handedly countless codebases both professionally and academically, and got into GUI dev too (Java Swing/FX and Android).

This was pretty much the start of my engineering career. From there I had several other jobs where I improved on my software development skills, general engineering skills (systems, mechanical, optical, and electrical), and leadership skills. To where I find myself today, facilitating the design and development of software by: inspiring people to ask questions, understand the problem and solution thoroughly; architecting the best system through research and trade studies; and developing software with clean code principles. All while engaging and communicating effectively with stakeholders, project/program managers, and team members to ensure projects run timely and successfully.

In parallel to my professional growth, I have been in college since 2010. First obtaining my bachelor's in EE (2015), then my master's in EE (2018), and embarking on a journey toward a PhD in ECE (starting 2019). I estimate I'm about 90% complete with my PhD at this point, having completed all coursework and exams. I'm currently in a research/publishing phase with only a journal paper requirement remaining, and then it's on to dissertation defense. Graduation is expected by the end of spring term 2027.

Aside from work and school, my hobbies have morphed over the years, but since I spend so much time programming and designing boards for work and school, I've become a bit of a gear head during my free time. So I work on my cars and motorcycles. I like that it's technical, keeps me away from the screen, and is somewhat physical. Aside from this, I also enjoy fixing things around the house, and learning about philosophy, economics, and finance.
